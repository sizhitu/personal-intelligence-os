# Learning, Positive Feedback & Compounding Loop

## Purpose

Turn verified good decisions into repeatable capabilities and durable whole-system gains. The system should not merely avoid failure; it should identify which mechanisms create value, validate them across contexts, and scale them without destroying the conditions that made them work.

**Core principle:** amplify a validated mechanism, not a lucky outcome or a vanity metric.

## The seven-stage loop

1. **Pre-register the success hypothesis.** Before action, record the intended whole-system outcome, causal hypothesis, assumptions, system boundary, affected stakeholders, leading indicators, lagging indicators, time horizon, expected costs, and explicit stop/rollback conditions. Preserve the baseline and a reasonable comparison where feasible.
2. **Measure the actual outcome.** Compare observed results with the baseline and forecast. Include intended and unintended effects, quality, trust, resilience, opportunity cost, and effects across Faith, Family, Business, and Life where relevant. Never infer whole-system success from one local KPI.
3. **Attribute carefully.** Separate decision quality, execution quality, external conditions, timing, and luck. Ask what would likely have happened without the intervention. Use comparison groups, repeated observations, or counterfactual reasoning where feasible; disclose when causal attribution is weak.
4. **Extract the mechanism.** Identify necessary conditions, sufficient-looking but unproven conditions, key actions, bottlenecks, reinforcing loops, limiting loops, and failure boundaries. Preserve contrary evidence and failed replications. Distinguish a causal mechanism from a post-hoc story.
5. **Replicate at small scale.** Test the mechanism in another period, cohort, customer segment, team, or context while changing as few variables as practical. Do not scale materially until repeatability and guardrails are demonstrated at a level appropriate to the stakes.
6. **Scale in stages.** Increase scope, budget, time, automation, or responsibility incrementally. At each stage, compare marginal benefit with marginal cost and risk; check capacity, quality, stakeholder impact, concentration, operational complexity, and whether the original mechanism still holds. Scaling should be reversible where possible.
7. **Monitor, adapt, and compound.** Track leading and lagging indicators, drift, diminishing returns, external changes, and unintended consequences. Continue, expand, hold, redesign, or roll back based on pre-agreed triggers. Capture reusable playbooks and propose methodology updates for user approval.

## Evidence gates before amplification

Use evidence tiers rather than a universal fixed sample count:
- **E0 — Anecdotal:** one observed success; record it, but do not generalize or materially scale.
- **E1 — Plausible:** mechanism is coherent and evidence is consistent, but confounding remains; run a small replication.
- **E2 — Replicated:** results recur in more than one relevant setting or period, with key assumptions and boundary conditions documented; cautious staged scaling may be considered.
- **E3 — Robust:** multiple replications, credible comparisons or strong causal evidence, stable guardrails, and stress tests support scaling; larger expansion may be considered with monitoring.

These are governance labels, not statistical certifications. Sample size, duration, and evidence design must fit the decision domain, base rate, effect size, cost, and risk. For rare high-impact events, absence of observed harm is not proof of safety.

## Scale-up gates and brakes

Before each expansion, check:
- hard constraints remain satisfied: user-defined Faith commitments, law, safety, consent, and explicit non-negotiables;
- whole-system outcomes remain positive, including family responsibilities and life sustainability where relevant;
- marginal returns remain attractive after hidden costs and opportunity costs;
- the process has enough capacity, quality control, redundancy, and recovery capability;
- the causal mechanism still applies at the larger scale;
- concentration, dependency, fragility, and externalities have not increased beyond acceptable limits;
- leading indicators and rollback controls are observable and actionable.

Pause, reduce, redesign, or reverse when a hard constraint is breached, key indicators deteriorate, costs are displaced, the mechanism no longer applies, or downside exceeds the agreed tolerance. Do not let sunk costs or prior success justify continued expansion.

## Positive feedback without runaway growth

A reinforcing loop can compound value, but it can also amplify errors. For every proposed flywheel, document:
- reinforcing loop: how a gain creates resources or conditions for further gains;
- balancing loop: what naturally limits growth or restores stability;
- delay: how long until benefits and harms become visible;
- saturation: where marginal returns decline;
- failure propagation: how a small defect could spread at scale;
- control lever: what can slow, isolate, or reverse the loop.

The goal is a **healthy reinforcing loop with functioning balancing controls**, not growth at any cost.

## Decision review record

For each review, capture:
- decision ID, review date, review horizon, and reviewer;
- original objective, system boundary, baseline, forecast, and success hypothesis;
- actual outcomes by metric and stakeholder/domain;
- leading/lagging indicators and data provenance;
- judgment / execution / environment / luck attribution with confidence;
- counterfactual and alternative explanations;
- mechanism, necessary conditions, boundary conditions, and counterevidence;
- local KPI versus whole-system outcome;
- evidence tier and replication status;
- next stage and scale amount/scope, or hold / stop / rollback decision;
- monitoring cadence, owner, thresholds, and next review date;
- proposed playbook/methodology change, approval status, and version.

## Governance

- One win never automatically changes general methodology or triggers aggressive scaling.
- Do not cherry-pick successful examples; preserve failed attempts and denominator data.
- Avoid survivorship bias, regression-to-the-mean errors, selection bias, and post-hoc storytelling.
- Do not optimize a proxy once it becomes a target; keep outcome measures and guardrails distinct.
- Keep user values and hard constraints outside compensatory scores.
- Record domain-specific permissions and consent before using customer, team, or family data.
- User approval is required for changes to core values and general methodology.

## Relationship to global optimization

This loop is subordinate to the [Global Optimization Guard](GLOBAL_OPTIMIZATION_GUARD.md). A successful local mechanism must pass whole-system checks before replication and at every scale-up stage. Scaling is rejected if it improves a local metric while degrading long-term system health, violating a hard constraint, or imposing unacceptable costs on another stakeholder or domain.


## Intelligent failure and disciplined experimentation

The learning loop must also treat well-designed failed tests as possible progress. Apply [Experimentation & Intelligent Failure](EXPERIMENTATION_AND_INTELLIGENT_FAILURE.md): pre-register hypotheses, anticipate failure modes, cap exposure, define stop/rollback conditions, and specify what each outcome would teach. Classify results as informative, preventable, or poorly designed. Celebrate honest, bounded learning—not damage, recklessness, or repeated avoidable mistakes. A failed test may justify another small experiment, never automatic escalation.
