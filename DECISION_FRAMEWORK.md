# Decision Framework

## Workflow
1. Define the decision, outcome, and deadline.
2. Clarify values, domain, constraints, and stakeholders.
3. Separate facts, assumptions, unknowns, and opinions.
4. Classify stakes, uncertainty, reversibility, complexity, and time pressure.
5. Choose fast or slow thinking effort proportionate to stakes.
6. Select a small set of relevant models and explain why.
7. Gather evidence with source, date, reliability, and counterevidence.
8. Generate alternatives, including status quo and staged options.
9. Compare trade-offs, opportunity cost, second-order effects, and downside.
10. Run a cognitive-trap checklist and pre-mortem.
11. Recommend action, safeguards, confidence, and conditions that would change the view.
12. Record the user's decision, rationale, and next action.
13. Review outcomes and distinguish judgment, execution, and luck.

## Risk tiers
- **Low:** reversible, low cost, familiar → lightweight analysis.
- **Moderate:** meaningful trade-offs or uncertainty → evidence, alternatives, and risk checks.
- **High:** large downside, long-term impact, hard to reverse, or health/legal/financial/family stakes → slow thinking, independent evidence, scenarios, safeguards, explicit uncertainty, and professional input where appropriate.

This is decision support, not a substitute for professional advice in regulated or high-stakes domains.

## Recommendation format
Situation and objective; values; facts and evidence; assumptions and unknowns; problem classification; selected models and rationale; options and trade-offs; risks and safeguards; recommendation and confidence; what could change the view; next action and review trigger.

## Learning rules
Do not change a method based on one outcome. Consider sample size, base rates, uncertainty, execution quality, luck, and counterevidence. Methodology changes are proposals requiring user approval.
