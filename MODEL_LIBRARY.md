# Mental Model Library — Initial Taxonomy

This is a curated starting taxonomy, not a claim that every model is universally valid. Store each model as a versioned record with sources, context, limitations, and examples.

## 1. Cognitive psychology and judgment
- System 1 / System 2 (Kahneman): intuitive vs effortful reasoning.
- Anchoring; availability heuristic; representativeness and base-rate neglect.
- Confirmation bias and motivated reasoning.
- Overconfidence; planning fallacy; loss aversion; framing effects.
- Hindsight bias; affect heuristic; cognitive dissonance.
- In-group, authority, halo, and status-quo biases.
- Sunk-cost fallacy; regression to the mean.

## 2. Charlie Munger / multidisciplinary thinking
- Inversion: ask how the outcome could fail.
- Incentives: identify what rewards make people do.
- Opportunity cost; circle of competence; margin of safety.
- Second-order thinking; latticework of models.
- Lollapalooza effect: interacting incentives and biases.
- Comparative advantage; specialization; compounding.

## 3. Scientific method and epistemology
- Hypothesis → prediction → test → update.
- Falsifiability and disconfirming evidence.
- Bayesian updating and likelihood; base rates and reference classes.
- Correlation vs causation; controlled comparison.
- Measurement validity and operational definitions.
- Reproducibility; independent verification; Occam's razor (not proof).
- Steelman competing explanations; separate observation, inference, interpretation.

## 4. Systems thinking and cybernetics
- System boundary; stocks and flows.
- Reinforcing and balancing feedback loops.
- Delays, lagging indicators, nonlinearity, thresholds, emergence.
- Leverage points; Goodhart's law / metric gaming.
- Ashby's law of requisite variety.
- Control loop: goal → observe → compare → act → feedback.
- Adaptation, robustness, resilience; local vs system-wide optimization.

## 5. Risk, uncertainty, and decision theory
- Expected value (with caveats); expected utility.
- Probability distributions and confidence intervals.
- Scenario analysis, stress testing, sensitivity analysis.
- Fat tails; ruin probability and survival.
- Margin of safety; optionality and real options.
- Reversibility and staged commitment; pre-mortem.
- Redundancy and diversification; value of information.
- Regret minimization; robust decisions under deep uncertainty.
- Kelly criterion as theory, never an automatic position-sizing instruction.

## 6. Economics, strategy, and organizations
- Marginal thinking; incentives; principal-agent problems.
- Comparative advantage; transaction costs.
- Network effects; switching costs; moat.
- Game theory, repeated games, cooperation and trust.
- Competitive dynamics; scale economies/diseconomies.
- Prisoner's dilemma; Goodhart's law in goal-setting.

## 7. Reasoning traps checklist
Use these as diagnostic prompts; distinguish cognitive biases from formal logical fallacies and reconcile duplicates:
anchoring, availability, confirmation bias, base-rate neglect, survivorship bias, selection bias, outcome bias, hindsight bias, overconfidence, planning fallacy, sunk-cost fallacy, loss aversion, framing effect, status quo bias, endowment effect, authority bias, social proof/herding, halo effect, false consensus, fundamental attribution error, self-serving bias, Dunning–Kruger (use cautiously), regression-to-mean neglect, gambler's fallacy, hot-hand fallacy, narrative fallacy, illusion of control, present bias, zero-risk bias, action bias, omission bias, scope neglect, conjunction fallacy, ecological fallacy, correlation-causation confusion, false dichotomy, straw man, ad hominem, appeal to nature/authority, cherry-picking, moving goalposts, motte-and-bailey, Texas sharpshooter, no-true-Scotsman, overgeneralization, unsupported slippery slope, weak analogy, argument from ignorance, begging the question, availability cascade.

This is a working checklist, not a definitive canonical “52” list. Link entries to sources and explain conditions where each trap may or may not apply.
