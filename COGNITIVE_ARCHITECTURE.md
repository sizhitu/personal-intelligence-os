# Cognitive Architecture

Personal Intelligence OS is a personal decision system, not a static quote or book library.

## Four layers
1. **Model Library** — source-linked mental models, principles, methods, cognitive traps, risk tools, and historical cases.
2. **Model Router** — classifies problem type, stakes, uncertainty, and proposes a small explainable set of models.
3. **Decision Engine** — derives decision-specific factors from selected models, proposes explainable adaptive weights, evaluates options, retrieves historical cases, and produces a decision brief.
4. **Personal Methodology Loop** — captures decisions and outcomes, tests lessons, and proposes (never silently makes) changes to personal principles.

## Model-driven adaptive weighting
There is no universal fixed weight vector. The Model Router selects models based on the decision context; those models contribute relevant criteria and diagnostic questions. The system merges duplicates, applies hard constraints, proposes relative weights with explanations, evaluates options separately, and tests sensitivity. See DECISION_FACTOR_WEIGHTS.md.

## Case-Based Reasoning layer
Every substantive decision should, after presenting its conclusion and rationale, retrieve relevant historical cases where reliable examples exist. Explain mechanism-level similarity, lessons, and limits. Cases enrich reasoning; they do not prove conclusions.

## Intellectual foundations
- **Cybernetics and Scientific Methodology**: system observation, control, feedback, information filtering, evolution, and black-box epistemology.
- **Thinking, Fast and Slow**: judgment mechanisms and cognitive biases.
- **Poor Charlie's Almanack**: multidisciplinary models, inversion, incentives, opportunity cost, second-order thinking.
- **The Art of Thinking Clearly**: prompts for reasoning traps.
- **Scientific method / Bayesian reasoning**: hypothesis testing, evidence, uncertainty, updating, falsification.
- **Risk and decision theory**: downside, reversibility, optionality, robust choices.

## Decision cycle
Observe → define decision → clarify values and constraints → classify problem → select models → extract/weight factors → gather evidence → generate alternatives → challenge preferred option → retrieve and compare historical cases → assess risk and reversibility → decide → act → review → propose learning.

## Governance
Distinguish facts, user-confirmed values, assumptions, hypotheses, and unknowns. No model, score, or case may override user values. Fast thinking can serve familiar, low-stakes, reversible choices; slow thinking is triggered by high stakes, irreversibility, unfamiliarity, emotional pressure, conflicting evidence, low confidence, cognitive traps, or tail risk. Track decision quality, execution, outcome, luck, calibration, model applicability, and falsifying evidence separately. Changes to values or core methodology require explicit user approval.
