# Cognitive Architecture

Personal Intelligence OS is a personal decision system, not a static quote or book library.

## Four layers
1. **Model Library** — source-linked mental models, principles, methods, cognitive traps, and risk tools.
2. **Model Router** — classifies problem type, stakes, uncertainty, and proposes a small explainable set of models.
3. **Decision Engine** — combines values, evidence, alternatives, downside, and model insights into a decision brief.
4. **Personal Methodology Loop** — captures decisions and outcomes, tests lessons, and proposes (never silently makes) changes to personal principles.

## Case-Based Reasoning layer
Every substantive decision should, after presenting its conclusion and rationale, retrieve a small set of relevant historical cases or precedent-like examples. It must explain the mechanism-level similarity, the lesson, and the limits of the analogy. Case references enrich reasoning; they do not replace evidence or prove a conclusion.

Distinguish user-personal cases, authorized team/organization cases, verified public historical cases, empirical research cases, and clearly labeled synthetic examples. Never present a fabricated or illustrative scenario as a real historical event.

## Intellectual foundations
- **Cybernetics and Scientific Methodology** (Jin Guantao / Hua Guofan): system observation, control, feedback, information filtering, evolution, and black-box epistemology.
- **Thinking, Fast and Slow**: judgment mechanisms and cognitive biases.
- **Poor Charlie's Almanack**: multidisciplinary models, inversion, incentives, opportunity cost, second-order thinking.
- **The Art of Thinking Clearly**: prompts for reasoning traps.
- **Scientific method / Bayesian reasoning**: hypothesis testing, evidence, uncertainty, updating, falsification.
- **Risk and decision theory**: downside, reversibility, optionality, robust choices.

## Decision cycle
Observe → define decision → clarify values and constraints → classify problem → select models → gather evidence → generate alternatives → challenge preferred option → retrieve and compare historical cases → assess risk and reversibility → decide → act → review → propose learning.

## Model and case selection
Use the smallest useful set. Explain applicability, contribution, and limits. For high-stakes decisions, require independent evidence, base rates, downside analysis, counterexamples, and explicit uncertainty. Distinguish facts, user-confirmed values, assumptions, hypotheses, and unknowns. No model or case may override user values.

## Fast and slow thinking
Fast thinking can serve familiar, low-stakes, reversible choices. Trigger slow thinking for high stakes, irreversibility, unfamiliarity, emotional pressure, conflicting evidence, low confidence, cognitive traps, or tail risk. Match effort to stakes.

## Learning and calibration
Track decision quality, execution, outcome, luck, calibration, model applicability, and falsifying evidence separately. Do not update methodology from a single outcome. Core values and methodology changes require explicit user approval.
